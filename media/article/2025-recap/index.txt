41:T69da,<h1 id="consolidation-as-logos">Consolidation as Logos</h1><p>Our movement’s biggest development last year was the unification of Logos technologies under a single identity: Logos.&nbsp;</p><p>Revealed in November, the decision came on the back of community feedback: you told us our previous naming structure was hard to follow and fragmented attention. So, we changed it.</p><p>The&nbsp;<a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://logos.co/tech-stack">unified stack</a> consists of the following components:</p><ul><li>Blockchain (formerly Nomos):&nbsp;</li><li>Messaging (formerly Waku).</li><li>Storage (formerly Codex).</li></ul><p>We’ve begun winding down the online presence of the above subbrands. Their social media accounts have been renamed and we'll no longer be pushing technical updates through them.&nbsp;</p><p>Instead, we’ll be using the unified&nbsp;<a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://x.com/Logos_devs">Logos Tech account</a>,&nbsp;which will also host various vibe coding and other developer-focused sessions going forward.&nbsp;Similarly, the individual protocol websites now redirect to the&nbsp;<a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://logos.co/">Logos homepage</a>.</p><p>For updates regarding Logos’ social movement activities, we’ll continue to use the main&nbsp;<a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://x.com/Logos_network">Logos account</a>.&nbsp;</p><h2 id="new-visual-identity">New visual identity</h2><p><img alt="Tech-Meta-logos-2025-update.png" src="/media-images/Tech_Meta_logos_2025_update_5450d1f6ee-e8a302ec-750.webp" srcset="/media-images/Tech_Meta_logos_2025_update_5450d1f6ee-e8a302ec-750.webp 750w, /media-images/Tech_Meta_logos_2025_update_5450d1f6ee-e8a302ec-1200.webp 1200w" sizes="(max-width: 767px) calc(100vw - 32px), 700px" width="1200" height="630" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></p><p>Along with the tech stack’s consolidation under a unified identity, we took the opportunity to update our movement’s visual identity. Our new design language is more open, inviting, and optimistic.&nbsp;</p><p>Logos is still highly aligned with the cypherpunk ideals that inspired our original look and feel. However, if we want our ideas and technologies to improve the lives of as many people as possible, we need to appeal to the broadest audience possible. Our new imagery enables us to do that.</p><p>You’ve probably already noticed our new logo, too. We chose the lambda character from the Greek alphabet because:</p><ul><li>It’s the Greek letter “L”, which is the first letter of “Logos”.</li><li>It’s used to talk about logical thinking in philosophy.</li><li>In computer science, it stands for functions that don’t have a name.</li><li>It’s a Unicode symbol, so it can be used almost anywhere, like this: λ.</li></ul><p><a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://logos.co/">Check the changes on the Logos website</a>, which also had a complete rework as part of November’s consolidation. While Balaji argued for the network state as a new organising model, Jarrad and Peter challenged the concept, favouring the idea of blockchain communities (as they discuss in&nbsp;<a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://logos.co/farewell-to-westphalia"><i>Farewell to Westphalia</i></a>) and questioning whether diplomatic recognition or state-like legitimacy is either necessary or desirable.</p><p>Privacy and resistance to surveillance were recurring threads throughout the year. Aaron Day joined twice to discuss how technocratic surveillance regimes operate and to share practical ways individuals can reclaim privacy online. Additional conversations with Michel Bauwens, Paul Puey, and contributors from DarkFi explored cosmolocalism, self-custody, wallet-level privacy, and the cultural and technical strategies needed to build resilient parallel systems.</p><h1 id="technical-highlights">Technical highlights</h1><p>Following Logos' consolidation, development focused on aligning Logos into a single stack, optimised for builders, and preparing the launch of the public testnet (expected in early 2026).</p><p>A key outcome was Logos Core, a modular runtime that exposes messaging, storage, and blockchain functionality through composable modules rather than a monolithic client.</p><p>Messaging continued to mature through production use and protocol refinement. Storage paused its existing testnet while the protocol was reworked to deliver reliable node-side storage and retrieval aligned with the integrated stack. Blockchain development advanced on core foundations, including consensus, data availability, and the Logos State Separation Architecture (LSSA), ahead of the testnet launch later this year.</p><p>Further technical detail is covered in the <a href="/media/article/2025-dev-retrospective">full technical developer update on Logos Press Engine</a>.</p><figure class="image"><img style="aspect-ratio:2441/1844;" alt="Logos-nov-update-tech-stack.jpg" src="/media-images/Logos_nov_update_tech_stack_a8c5adda0f-3ec79cd4-750.webp" srcset="/media-images/Logos_nov_update_tech_stack_a8c5adda0f-3ec79cd4-750.webp 750w, /media-images/Logos_nov_update_tech_stack_a8c5adda0f-3ec79cd4-1400.webp 1400w" sizes="(max-width: 767px) calc(100vw - 32px), 700px" width="2441" height="1844" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure><h1 id="celebrating-community-champions">Celebrating community champions</h1><p>Logos is passionately open source and the product of the people who believe in it.
