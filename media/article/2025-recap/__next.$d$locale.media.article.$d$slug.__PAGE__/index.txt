11:T69da,<h1 id="consolidation-as-logos">Consolidation as Logos</h1><p>Our movement’s biggest development last year was the unification of Logos technologies under a single identity: Logos.&nbsp;</p><p>Revealed in November, the decision came on the back of community feedback: you told us our previous naming structure was hard to follow and fragmented attention. So, we changed it.</p><p>The&nbsp;<a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://logos.co/tech-stack">unified stack</a> consists of the following components:</p><ul><li>Blockchain (formerly Nomos):&nbsp;</li><li>Messaging (formerly Waku).</li><li>Storage (formerly Codex).</li></ul><p>We’ve begun winding down the online presence of the above subbrands. Their social media accounts have been renamed and we'll no longer be pushing technical updates through them.&nbsp;</p><p>Instead, we’ll be using the unified&nbsp;<a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://x.com/Logos_devs">Logos Tech account</a>,&nbsp;which will also host various vibe coding and other developer-focused sessions going forward.&nbsp;Similarly, the individual protocol websites now redirect to the&nbsp;<a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://logos.co/">Logos homepage</a>.</p><p>For updates regarding Logos’ social movement activities, we’ll continue to use the main&nbsp;<a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://x.com/Logos_network">Logos account</a>.&nbsp;</p><h2 id="new-visual-identity">New visual identity</h2><p><img alt="Tech-Meta-logos-2025-update.png" src="/media-images/Tech_Meta_logos_2025_update_5450d1f6ee-e8a302ec-750.webp" srcset="/media-images/Tech_Meta_logos_2025_update_5450d1f6ee-e8a302ec-750.webp 750w, /media-images/Tech_Meta_logos_2025_update_5450d1f6ee-e8a302ec-1200.webp 1200w" sizes="(max-width: 767px) calc(100vw - 32px), 700px" width="1200" height="630" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></p><p>Along with the tech stack’s consolidation under a unified identity, we took the opportunity to update our movement’s visual identity. Our new design language is more open, inviting, and optimistic.&nbsp;</p><p>Logos is still highly aligned with the cypherpunk ideals that inspired our original look and feel. However, if we want our ideas and technologies to improve the lives of as many people as possible, we need to appeal to the broadest audience possible. Our new imagery enables us to do that.</p><p>You’ve probably already noticed our new logo, too. We chose the lambda character from the Greek alphabet because:</p><ul><li>It’s the Greek letter “L”, which is the first letter of “Logos”.</li><li>It’s used to talk about logical thinking in philosophy.</li><li>In computer science, it stands for functions that don’t have a name.</li><li>It’s a Unicode symbol, so it can be used almost anywhere, like this: λ.</li></ul><p><a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://logos.co/">Check the changes on the Logos website</a>, which also had a complete rework as part of November’s consolidation. Let us know what you think of our new look and feel&nbsp;<a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://forum.logos.co/t/logos-is-entering-a-new-chapter/1437">on the Logos Forum</a>.</p><h1 id="logos-testnet-and-node-programme">Logos testnet and node programme</h1><p>With a new, consolidated identity came another big announcement: the Logos testnet is coming in Q1 2026.&nbsp;</p><p>The testnet will be the first chance for developers to experiment with and build on Logos’ integrated technology framework. Ahead of its release, we’ve introduced a node programme to help those wanting to run hardware to strengthen the Logos network to get started.&nbsp;</p><p>You can expect more details about both the testnet and the node programme very soon. But if you’d like to operate a node at testnet launch,&nbsp;<a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://logos.co/node-programme">sign up for the programme today</a>.&nbsp;</p><h1 id="logos-circles-tackling-local-issues-with-logos-tech">Logos Circles: Tackling local issues with Logos tech</h1><p><img alt="logos-may-lisbon-circle-2025-recap.jpg" src="/media-images/logos_may_lisbon_circle_2025_recap_25dd0d85aa-56aa09f1-750.webp" srcset="/media-images/logos_may_lisbon_circle_2025_recap_25dd0d85aa-56aa09f1-750.webp 750w, /media-images/logos_may_lisbon_circle_2025_recap_25dd0d85aa-56aa09f1-1138.webp 1138w" sizes="(max-width: 767px) calc(100vw - 32px), 700px" width="1138" height="640" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></p><p>Last year, Logos began applying our ideas and technologies to real-world problems around the globe through the&nbsp;<a href="/media/article/logos-circles-winnable-issues">Logos Circles initiative</a>. Across cities and continents, Circles have brought people together to focus on&nbsp;<i>winnable issues</i>: local problems that are concrete, achievable, and that improve the lives of real people.</p><p>Winnable issues are problems that are small enough to be solved but significant enough to matter. Rather than aiming immediately at large, abstract change, Circles focus on areas where a group of motivated people can realistically make a positive impact, building momentum through early, shared successes.</p><p>From zero local groups at the start of 2025, regular meetups are now happening in 23 cities around the world.&nbsp;</p><p>We’re actively looking for stewards to lead Logos Circles in new territories. If you want to lead a Logos Circle in your area, you can&nbsp;<a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://cal.com/team/logos-onboarding/intro">schedule a call with us to discuss your plans</a>. We’ll support you with best practices, promotion, and guidance.</p><h2 id="circle-wins-in-2025">Circle wins in 2025</h2><ul><li><strong>Lisbon</strong>: Built a long-term partnership with the culturally rich but historically marginalised Quinta do Mocho neighbourhood, securing a community centre for youth workshops, organising winter clothing and gift drives, and producing a short film on community life and grassroots organising (premiering 6 March at&nbsp;<a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://ps.logos.co/">Parallel Society</a>).</li><li><strong>Zanzibar:</strong> Supported the Straight Training Center by launching a donations website, raising $2,000 for 10 Raspberry Pi computers, and helping secure $1,500 to cover another year of rent for the community-run school.&nbsp;<a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://donate.straighttraining.center/">Check it out and donate if you can spare a little</a>.</li><li><strong>London:</strong> Laid the groundwork for a public knowledge hub on digital ID risks, including launching a coordination email group, engaging directly with policymakers at a House of Lords roundtable, and beginning a whitepaper to inform future government debates.</li><li><strong>Los Angeles:</strong> Advanced plans for a privacy-first neighbourhood engagement app (ZK Nextdoor), drafting core features and technical architecture and beginning work towards a prototype.</li><li><strong>Porto:</strong> Made progress towards Europe’s first digital escape and egress tools for domestic violence survivors, collaborating with privacy and safety partners, preparing MVP websites for field testing, and exploring sustainable fundraising options.</li><li><strong>Benin:</strong> Began work on FundBrave, a regenerative social-DeFi fundraising platform, with early organising underway to support community-led funding and local economic resilience.</li><li><strong>Online:&nbsp;</strong>Our Online Circles serve as a regular forum to share updates and initiatives between individual Circles. Hosted every Tuesday on the&nbsp;<a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://x.com/Logos_network">Logos X account</a>, they’re a great way to learn more about and get involved with Circles.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;</li></ul><p>Other Logos Circles are still in their early stages, meeting regularly and identifying the winnable issues they'll focus on.</p><p><a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://circles.logos.co/">Learn more about Logos Circles</a>.</p><p><img alt="logos-circle-zan-2025-recap.jpeg" src="/media-images/logos_circle_zan_2025_recap_d3b058e6c8-aba3692f-750.webp" srcset="/media-images/logos_circle_zan_2025_recap_d3b058e6c8-aba3692f-750.webp 750w, /media-images/logos_circle_zan_2025_recap_d3b058e6c8-aba3692f-1400.webp 1400w" sizes="(max-width: 767px) calc(100vw - 32px), 700px" width="4096" height="2886" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></p><h1 id="privacy-in-practice-hacking-for-freedom-and-strengthening-allegiances">Privacy in Practice: Hacking for freedom and strengthening allegiances</h1><p>In September, we launched Privacy in Practice, an ongoing, multi-part campaign to encourage the development of practical privacy-focused tools that address real-world needs. The initiative allowed us to work closely with organisations that share our mission to develop technical solutions to defend rights and freedoms.&nbsp;</p><h2 id="realfi-hack">RealFi Hack</h2><p><img alt="logos-hackathon-realfi-winners-logos-25.png" src="/media-images/logos_hackathon_realfi_winners_logos_25_b91d8f1859-676b89fa-750.webp" srcset="/media-images/logos_hackathon_realfi_winners_logos_25_b91d8f1859-676b89fa-750.webp 750w, /media-images/logos_hackathon_realfi_winners_logos_25_b91d8f1859-676b89fa-1400.webp 1400w" sizes="(max-width: 767px) calc(100vw - 32px), 700px" width="5912" height="3692" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></p><p>We kicked off Privacy in Practice by sponsoring RealFi Hack, a virtual hackathon running from 23 September to 16 October in collaboration with the Tor Project, Internet Archive, Funding the Commons, and other aligned organisations. Several standout projects emerged from the Logos-sponsored tracks, including:</p><ul><li><strong>Ash:</strong> a peer-to-peer coordination tool for activists built on a decentralised messaging network.</li><li><strong>ComuniFi:</strong> a censorship-resistant communication and finance platform integrating messaging and onchain payments.</li><li><strong>Shielded Micropay:</strong> a privacy-preserving payment protocol for low-cost private transactions.</li><li><strong>Tohaku</strong>: a privacy-focused Ethereum wallet that routes interactions through Tor or mixnets to reduce surveillance risk.</li></ul><p><a href="/media/article/realfi-hack">Learn more about the RealFi Hack and the winning submissions</a>.</p><h2 id="buenos-aires">Buenos Aires</h2><p>Following the RealFi Hack, we took Privacy in Practice to Buenos Aires, the host city for this year’s Devconnect. Logos sponsored and participated in several initiatives designed to build connections, prototype tools, and foster collaboration around real-world privacy and public-goods infrastructure.<a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://dev.logos.co/campaigns/devconnect2025?utm_source=chatgpt.com">&nbsp;</a></p><p>We supported and attended the Funding the Commons Builder Residency, which ran from 24 October to 14 November. Logos contributors mentored participants and collaborated on Keycard-adjacent projects ahead of the upcoming launch of&nbsp;<a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://get.keycard.tech/pages/keycard-shell">Keycard Shell</a>, a major new development in modular self-custodial crypto storage from the <a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://free.technology/">Institute of Free Technology</a>.</p><p>Next, Logos supported and contributed to the Funding the Commons Conference, with talks, workshops, and interactive sessions, including a governance design workshop using Logos tooling. The event, held in Lisbon, Portugal, on 6 and 7 March 2026, invites aligned organisations and individuals to join the Logos community for a celebration of cultures, ideas, and solutions.</p><p>At the heart of the Logos movement is the idea of parallel organising: i.e. taking action to solve local problems that have been overlooked or ignored by existing institutional bodies. These conversations became a regular place to explore how new forms of coordination, governance, and community can emerge outside legacy institutions.</p><p>Key themes included exit and jurisdictional choice, digital surveillance and privacy, decentralised finance, and post-nation-state organising. In one standout discussion, Jarrad Hope joined the Space to unpack the idea of “exit”, examining its limits as well as its potential, and exploring how access, exile, and power shape the real-world feasibility of opting out.</p><p>Another highlight was a lively debate between Balaji Srinivasan, Peter Ludlow, and Jarrad Hope on the nature of emerging political forms. While Balaji argued for the network state as a new organising model, Jarrad and Peter challenged the concept, favouring the idea of blockchain communities (as they discuss in&nbsp;<a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://logos.co/farewell-to-westphalia"><i>Farewell to Westphalia</i></a>) and questioning whether diplomatic recognition or state-like legitimacy is either necessary or desirable.</p><p>Privacy and resistance to surveillance were recurring threads throughout the year.
