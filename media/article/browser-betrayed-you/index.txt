<h2>The signals that follow you</h2>

<div class="s0-pull-quote">Privacy is not a feature you activate at the moment of a transaction. It is a property of the entire system you inhabit — or it is nothing.</div>

<hr />

<h2>The environment is hostile by design</h2>

<p>The modern browser grants every website access to a staggering amount of system information. This is not an implementation flaw, it is a <em>design feature</em>. Websites need screen size for responsive layouts, GPU access for graphics, timezone for local times. Each API has a legitimate purpose. Collectively, they form a surveillance apparatus of remarkable precision.</p>

<p>Beyond the browser, wallet extensions introduce their own attack surface. MetaMask injects <code>window.ethereum</code> into every page — immediately identifying you as a crypto user before you click "Connect." MetaMask also has over 1,800 npm dependencies, and a single compromised package has the potential to exfiltrate private keys or redirect transactions. The risk is not theoretical: <a href="https://www.chainalysis.com/crypto-crime/">crypto theft through various attack vectors</a> regularly exceeds billions of dollars annually, with browser-based attacks — malicious extensions, supply chain compromises, session hijacking — accounting for a growing share.</p>

<p>This constant background data collection is the default state of the web. The question is not <em>whether</em> your browser activity is being correlated with your blockchain activity — it is how many entities have already done so.</p>

<h2>What can be done</h2>

<p>It would be dishonest to end this analysis with a clean list of mitigations that restore your privacy. The problem is structural. But there are measures you can take to mitigate your exposure:</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Use a privacy-focused browser:</strong> Tor Browser is the gold standard. For daily use, Firefox with <code>privacy.resistFingerprinting</code> and Brave with aggressive shields both meaningfully reduce fingerprint uniqueness. ${browserAdvice}</li>

<li><strong>Separate your identities:</strong> Use dedicated browser profiles — or better, separate browsers — for blockchain activity and general browsing. The cross-browser hardware signature we demonstrated shows why this is hard, but separation forces trackers to rely on probabilistic matching rather than deterministic linking.</li>

<li><strong>Run your own infrastructure:</strong> Use a local Ethereum node instead of Infura or Alchemy. Connect through a VPN or Tor. Self-hosting eliminates the most dangerous correlation vectors.</li>

<li><strong>Demand better infrastructure:</strong> The fundamental problem is architectural: the web assumes trust in a hostile environment. The long-term solution is infrastructure that does not make this assumption.</li>
</ul>
