1d:T2d5a,<h2 id="january-ecodev-roundup">January EcoDev Roundup</h2>
<p>Updates from the EcoDev team in January 2024, plus highlights from the first Waku community call.</p>
<p>The EcoDev team at Waku covers community, growth, partnerships, and communications. Each month, we will share the key highlights and progress of the team. You can <a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://waku.org/subscribe/">subscribe</a> to the monthly newsletter if you prefer to receive an abridged version of these updates in your inbox.</p>
<h3 id="events-and-hackathons">Events and Hackathons</h3>
<p>In 2023, Waku participated in three hackathons and eight events across four continents. We are gearing up for another year full of events; expect to see us all around the globe once again. Waku will be attending two key in-person events in Q1 2024. We will focus on growing the developer community and meeting with projects to support further integrations with Waku.</p>
<p><strong>ETHDenver 23rd February - 3rd March</strong></p>
<p>Meet the Waku team attending ETHDenver: Aaron Bendesrky, Waku Program Manager at Logos, Pedro Lains, Business Development Lead, and Guru T, Developer Relationships Engineer. ETHDenver is a great opportunity to meet the Waku team face to face; attendees can book an appointment with the team <a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://waku.org/events/eth-denver/">here</a>.</p>
<p><strong>ETHLatam 13th - 14th March</strong></p>
<p>Waku has $3,500 in bounties on offer during the hackathon. The team on the ground will support the hackers across the two days in building their projects for submission, and we expect to see many more new use cases for Waku.</p>
<p>Anybody hosting a side event at ETHDenver or ETHLatam and would like to collaborate with Waku, drop us message on <a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://discord.com/invite/gMPAzmcDER">Discord</a>.</p>
<h3 id="press-and-media">Press and Media</h3>
<p>**Vitalik Buterin Recognises Waku: **This month, Waku received an excellent shout-out from Vitalik Buterin, who recognised Waku as the successor of Whisper, the original peer-to-peer communications protocol for Ethereum. In a <a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2023/12/28/cypherpunk.html#ethereum-as-part-of-a-broader-technological-vision">blog post</a>, Buterin supported Waku as the realisation of the cypherpunk messaging protocol imagined by Gavin Wood.</p>
<p>The story was also covered in <a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://cointelegraph.com/press-releases/vitalik-buterin-recognises-waku-as-the-peer-to-peer-communications-layer-of-gavin-woods-web3-vision">CoinTelegraph</a>, <a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://cryptodaily.co.uk/2024/01/meet-waku-the-peer-to-peer-communications-protocol-recognized-by-vitalik-buterin">CryptoDaily</a>, and <a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://thedefiant.io/waku-the-p2p-communication-protocol-for-web3-recognized-by-vitalik-buterin">The Defiant</a>.</p>
<p><strong>Waku was voted as a top privacy project of 2023</strong>: Web3 Privacy Now included Waku on their long list of <a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://mirror.xyz/0x0f1F3DAf416B74DB3DE55Eb4D7513a80F4841073/LdOt0mlG_nXqhnmLf7s-X13NDNdpwQw72ESKaJq-0zA">top privacy-focused projects</a> of the year. The finalists were voted on by over 100 privacy enthusiasts, developers, researchers, cryptographers, cypherpunks, and community builders from DarkFi, Nym, Swarm, Logos, Railgun, and many other organisations.</p>
<h3 id="developer-resources">Developer Resources</h3>
<p><strong>The Waku React package:</strong> The package provides the components and UI adapters needed to integrate Waku SDK into React applications effortlessly. We developed the package in response to feedback from hackathon participants last year. The documentation, including detailed steps on using the package, <a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://docs.waku.org/guides/js-waku/use-waku-react/">can be found here</a>.</p>
<p>There is also a <a href="/media/article/tictactoe-tutorial">beginner-friendly tutorial</a> for building a simple tic-tac-toe game that can be played by two players who are connected using Waku.</p>
<p><strong>Open Bounties:</strong> We have reviewed and revised the Waku bounties, ensuring that these tasks are beginner-friendly. Our goal is to make it easier for newcomers to Waku to contribute and, in turn, support the growth of the ecosystem. Anyone can work on the bounties at their own pace, as there is no hard cut-off date. When a bounty proposal is approved, it will be marked as ‘Assigned’. Find the open bountie <a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://github.com/waku-org/bounties/issues">here</a>.</p>
<p><strong>Bounty Shipped:</strong> We are excited to announce a recent 1,000 DAI bounty shipped, awarded to Joel Adewole for a <a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://dev.to/wolzcodelife/build-a-dapp-using-waku-decentralized-communication-and-vuejs-1nnf">poll app built with Waku and Vue3</a>. The app is a spin-off of the popular platform Kahoot, with short-lived sessions and storage of the poll. Joel&#x27;s documentation is <a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://github.com/wolz-CODElife/waku-vue-poll?tab=readme-ov-file">available on GitHub</a>.</p>
<h3 id="team-updates">Team Updates</h3>
<p>The team has expanded this month, with the new EcoDev Team Lead, Marina and a new Business Development Lead, Pedro, joining the team.</p>
<p>You can connect with <a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://twitter.com/Crypto_marina">Marina</a> and <a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://twitter.com/bigbraincap">Pedro</a> on X.</p>
<p>If you are interested in working with Waku, <a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://boards.greenhouse.io/waku/jobs/5607301">we are currently hiring a Software Engineer</a> to work with the Waku Chat SDK. The position is fully remote, and we hire globally.</p>
<hr/>
<h3 id="waku-community-call-and-ama">Waku Community Call and AMA</h3>
<p>Waku will host a Community Call on X on the last Friday of each month. The objective of the call is to update the community on our progress and provide a space where questions can be put to the team</p>
<p>The first Community Call took place last week on the 26th of January. You can <a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://twitter.com/Waku_org/status/1750927368644919722">listen back to the full Space</a>, and read the highlights below.</p>
<p><a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://twitter.com/vpavlin">Vaclav</a>, <a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://twitter.com/hackyguru">Guru</a>, and <a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://twitter.com/vrycmfy">Aaron</a> from the Waku team participated in the call.</p>
<h4 id="waku-network-updates">Waku network updates</h4>
<p><strong>Vaclav</strong>: We are very proud to have released the Waku Network Gen 0 in December last year. If you don&#x27;t know what the Waku Network is, it is our attempt to formalise a network of peer-to-peer nodes that would form some network running Waku protocols.  It offers first-of-its-kind privacy-preserving DoS protections for peer-to-peer messaging.</p>
<p>It&#x27;s just the beginning of the Waku Network. We are drafting the <a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://github.com/waku-org/pm/blob/master/ROADMAP.md">2024 roadmap</a> with the research team, although it&#x27;s still subject to change. There’s also an interesting discussion you can join in the <a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://forum.vac.dev/t/message-validation-marketplace-waku-network-of-networks/262/2">Vac Forum</a>.</p>
<p>As part of Generation 0 of Waku Network, we have integrated sharding, ensuring that it’s scalable to thousands of users and that the bandwidth is reasonable for people running on Raspberry Pis or smaller devices with weaker connectivity. Our client targeted at low-resource devices, JSWaku, has been making progress towards fully supporting sharding and, by that, supporting the full Waku Network.</p>
<h4 id="future-incentivisation-for-running-waku-nodes">Future incentivisation for running Waku nodes?</h4>
<p>**Vaclav: **We are often asked about how we incentivise node runners, as well as who is running the nodes and why. There are currently no monetary or token incentives. However, the research team recently <a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://github.com/waku-org/research/blob/master/docs/incentivization.md">defined the problem</a> and is building the first PoC. That&#x27;s very interesting and promising for the future of Waku node operators to get something in return.</p>
<h4 id="growth-of-the-waku-dev-ecosystem">Growth of the Waku dev ecosystem</h4>
<p>**Guru: **We had our first Waku hackathon at ETHRome last year, where 120 hackers participated. We received eight project submissions from EthRome, which was a high number for us as a first-time sponsor. At ETHIstanbul our submissions increased to 20. We closed the year with ETHIndia where we had a huge total of 51 project submissions.</p>
<p>The graph has been trending consistently upwards as more hackers and developers discover Waku. We are exploring ways to offer support to these projects in terms of development, as well as raising funding and grants.</p>
<h4 id="project-integrations-with-waku">Project integrations with Waku</h4>
<p>**Guru: **Waku has been integrated into several existing projects already. <a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://status.app/">Status</a>, of course, is the original use case for Waku. We&#x27;re in the final stages of collaborating with Status to integrate Waku&#x27;s latest developments.</p>
<p><a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://www.railgun.org/">Railgun</a> has been friends of Waku for a long period of time, and they integrated Waku around a year and a half ago. We also completed an integration with <a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://thegraph.com/">The Graph</a>, which uses Waku in its Graphcast SDK to decentralise its indexes.</p>
<p><a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://www.silencelaboratories.com/">Silence Laboratories</a> has also been building a multiparty computation (MPC) on top of Waku.</p>
<h4 id="the-meaning-behind-the-name-waku">The meaning behind the name Waku</h4>
<p>**Vaclav: **The word Waku is Japanese meaning to well up, to gush forth, and to spring out.</p>
<p>Waku also has a meaning in Chewa language, a language spoken in Malawi. If we want to use the Chewa language meaning, it’s close to <em>keep your data local and your communication borderless</em>, which fits perfectly.</p>
