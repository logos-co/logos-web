f:T728c,<h2 id="logos-testnet-v0-3-release">Logos Testnet v0.3 release</h2><p>In September, Logos Testnet v0.3 was released, with improvements reaching almost every part of the stack, from how new participants join the network to how files and messages move between peers.</p><p>With EmPoWering live, participants can now get testnet tokens by performing computational work rather than requesting them centrally from a faucet. Logos Storage now allows downloads over Mix, and&nbsp;<a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://github.com/logos-co/logos-basecamp/releases/tag/0.3.1">Basecamp</a> added Windows support while also getting early versions of Ethereum and Monero wallets.</p><p>Other additions in Testnet v0.3 include Logos SQL, a distributed SQL database run over a blockchain channel, and Channel Notes, which allows tokens bridged on the base layer to be staked by a zone’s sequencers.</p><p><a href="/media/article/logos-testnet-v03-live#proof-of-work-participation-with-empowering"><i>Learn more about Testnet v0.3</i></a><i> /&nbsp;</i><a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://github.com/logos-co/logos-basecamp/releases/tag/0.3.1"><i>Run Basecamp and explore Testnet v0.3</i></a><i> /&nbsp;</i><a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://docs.logos.co/run-a-node"><i>Read the docs</i></a></p><h3 id="local-first-full-stack-privacy">Local-first full-stack privacy</h3><p>The Logos stack is engineered for truly private finance (PriFi). PriFi is a system design philosophy that aims to secure the entire transaction lifecycle – the steps involved in making any deal, transfer, or exchange that occur before and after settlement. Blockchain secures settlement uniquely well, but absolute transparency onchain introduces leaks at other links in the cycle.</p><figure class="image"><img style="aspect-ratio:3840/2160;" alt="Transaction_Supply_Chain_4.png" src="/media-images/Transaction_Supply_Chain_4_29113678d1-f1167298-750.webp" srcset="/media-images/Transaction_Supply_Chain_4_29113678d1-f1167298-750.webp 750w, /media-images/Transaction_Supply_Chain_4_29113678d1-f1167298-1400.webp 1400w" sizes="(max-width: 767px) calc(100vw - 32px), 700px" width="3840" height="2160" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure><p>Capital risks and the costs of protecting against them pose a barrier to entry for many market participants. PriFi infrastructure is engineered to disclose information only to necessary parties and shield by default. It runs P2P on users’ computers to minimise reliance on vulnerable proprietary hardware, while employing mechanisms to provide maximum privacy across the stack. By reducing costs and risks across the lifecycle, PriFi provides greater confidentiality and thus security than was previously attainable only by a wealthy few.</p><p><a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://logos.co/prifi"><i>Learn more about PriFi</i></a><i> /&nbsp;</i><a href="/media/article/pri-fi-securing-transaction-supply-chain"><i>Explore why PriFi matters&nbsp;</i></a><i>&nbsp;&nbsp;</i></p><h2 id="local-area-network-launches">Local Area Network launches</h2><p>September saw the relaunch of the ever-popular Logos zine. Local Area Network is a monthly snapshot of the movement as it flourishes. The first issue brings together tech updates, parallel organising news, Logos Circle win spotlights, a Monero thoughtpiece, and much more.&nbsp;</p><p>Local Area Network celebrates those who are building or acting on Logos, spotlighting wins to inspire and fork for use across the network. It is built collectively as an archive of all our successes and learnings.</p><figure class="image"><img style="aspect-ratio:518/204;" alt="logos-lan" src="/media-images/Screenshot_2026_10_03_at_15_31_28_LAN_Zine_Copy_lan_issue_01_pdf_34f526d08b-3f8d22c4-518.webp" srcset="/media-images/Screenshot_2026_10_03_at_15_31_28_LAN_Zine_Copy_lan_issue_01_pdf_34f526d08b-3f8d22c4-518.webp 518w" sizes="(max-width: 767px) calc(100vw - 32px), 700px" width="518" height="204" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure><p>For Local Area Network to be the true voice of the movement, it needs the thoughts of many. Send field reports, technical walkthroughs, essays, data, visuals, lessons learned, and working prototypes to&nbsp;<a href="mailto:pressengine@logos.co">pressengine@logos.co</a>.</p><p><a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://logos.co/zine/lan-issue-01.pdf"><i>Read Local Area Network: 01 The Founding Issue</i></a><i> /&nbsp;</i><a href="mailto:pressengine@logos.co"><i>Contribute to Local Area Network</i></a></p><h2 id="past-becomes-present-michael-and-amanda-s-stories">Past becomes Present: Michael and Amanda’s stories</h2><p>Past. Present. Future. is an initiative highlighting why infrastructure like Logos has never been more important and, if historic trends are anything to go by, is going to get a lot more important.</p><p>In Past, we went to the&nbsp;<a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://logos.co/past-present-future/museum-of-civil-liberties">Museum of Civil Liberties</a>, an Arctic archive of major events that resulted in encroachment of personal freedoms. In Present, we follow three very different stories: Michael, Amanda, and a still-unrevealed character. As the player takes each character’s major decisions, three very symptoms of a society in rapid decline emerge.&nbsp;</p><p>Michael’s story is about debt and how it restricts our choices. We join as he considers whether to borrow money to go to university or go straight into employment. Amanda’s story revolves around social media harms and how her health declines as her data is used against her.&nbsp;</p><p><a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://logos.co/past-present-future"><i>Play “Life’s Choices”</i></a><i> /&nbsp;</i><a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://logos.co/past-present-future/museum-of-civil-liberties"><i>Visit the Museum of Civil Liberties</i></a></p><figure class="image"><img style="aspect-ratio:1200/675;" alt="museum-civil-liberties-aug-logos-update.jpg" src="/media-images/museum_civil_liberties_aug_logos_update_3b829f6984-b047a889-750.webp" srcset="/media-images/museum_civil_liberties_aug_logos_update_3b829f6984-b047a889-750.webp 750w, /media-images/museum_civil_liberties_aug_logos_update_3b829f6984-b047a889-1200.webp 1200w" sizes="(max-width: 767px) calc(100vw - 32px), 700px" width="1200" height="675" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure><h2 id="building-the-parallel-with-ethereum-philippines">Building the parallel with Ethereum Philippines</h2><p>Last month, Ethereum Philippines teamed up with Logos for a hands-on technical workshop, “Building the Parallel”, led by Alisher. The session introduced attendees to the Logos stack, the design process behind it, and practical ways to start building on Logos.</p><p>Those watching learned how to run a Logos node, install Basecamp, and support the tech’s development through opportunities like RFPs and λPrizes.</p><figure class="image"><img style="aspect-ratio:800/800;" alt="Ethphilipines.jpeg" src="/media-images/Ethphilipines_e3baa1b582-07bb55f5-750.webp" srcset="/media-images/Ethphilipines_e3baa1b582-07bb55f5-750.webp 750w, /media-images/Ethphilipines_e3baa1b582-07bb55f5-800.webp 800w" sizes="(max-width: 767px) calc(100vw - 32px), 700px" width="800" height="800" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure><h2 id="irl-upcoming">IRL / Upcoming</h2><h3 id="freequencies">Freequencies</h3><figure class="image"><img style="aspect-ratio:400/400;" alt="freeq.jpg" src="/media-images/freeq_528ab1d979-21deacfc-400.webp" srcset="/media-images/freeq_528ab1d979-21deacfc-400.webp 400w" sizes="(max-width: 767px) calc(100vw - 32px), 700px" width="400" height="400" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure><p>Logos is supporting the launch of Freequences in Lisbon on 7 October. The three-month programme of gatherings brings artists, performers, technologists, and collectives together to share skills, collaborate, and build stronger connections across the local scene.&nbsp;</p><p>Freequencies is developing shared tools for coordination, archiving, communication, and event organisation on the Logos stack. The aim is to create infrastructure that Freequencies can use itself, while also enabling other cultural collectives to adopt, adapt, and self-host similar tools without relying on proprietary hardware controlled by Big Tech platforms.</p><p><a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://luma.com/logos-freequencies"><i>Join us in Lisbon on 7 October</i></a></p><h3 id="field-station-rajasthan">Field Station / Rajasthan</h3><figure class="image"><img style="aspect-ratio:776/437;" alt="fieldstation-logos-sept-update.png" src="/media-images/fieldstation_logos_sept_update_980dc662cc-5f99a981-750.webp" srcset="/media-images/fieldstation_logos_sept_update_980dc662cc-5f99a981-750.webp 750w, /media-images/fieldstation_logos_sept_update_980dc662cc-5f99a981-776.webp 776w" sizes="(max-width: 767px) calc(100vw - 32px), 700px" width="776" height="437" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure><p>Field Station / Rajasthan opened applications in September and will bring together 30 residents for an eight-day builder-and-activist residency at Dhun, Rajasthan, from 23–31 October. Created as a collaborative effort by Logos and Zu-Grama, the programme invites developers, researchers, founders, and activists to cohabit while building and experimenting with social coordination on decentralised infrastructure.</p><p>Residents will work across two tracks: the Logos sovereignty and privacy stack, and Zu-Grama’s d/acc track, spanning AI, neurotech and bio-resilience. The residency is designed around practical outcomes, with participants running infrastructure, building prototypes, and tackling real civic challenges, including privacy-preserving mutual aid and local reporting.</p><p>The programme will culminate in a summit and showcase on 31 October, where residents will share what they built during the week. Applications are open to both technical and non-technical participants interested in contributing to a hands-on experiment in building more resilient, local-first communities.</p><p><a href="/media/article/field-station-rajasthan"><i>Learn more about Field Station</i></a><i> /&nbsp;</i><a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://logos.co/field-station"><i>Apply to Field Station</i></a></p><h2 id="irl-september">IRL / September</h2><h3 id="dark-prague">Dark Prague</h3><p>From 2–4 October, Logos attended Dark Prague, bringing a three-day programme built around privacy, parallel infrastructure, and hands-on participation. Rather than treating it as a conventional conference, Logos created a working space where attendees could install Basecamp, spin up nodes, troubleshoot technical issues, explore PriFi, and contribute directly to the stack.</p><p>The programme combined technical workshops with Logos Circles, talks, and discussions on parallel organising, privacy infrastructure, and community-led alternatives to centralised systems. Attendees were encouraged not just to learn about the ideas behind Logos, but to leave having done something practical: running infrastructure, contributing to development, joining a Circle, or helping to start a new one.&nbsp;</p><p>Jarrad Hope, Logos cofounder, also presented “Vaults of Glass”, in which he explored blockchain as an incomplete institutional breakthrough. Blockchain is uniquely strong on settlement but weak at protecting the six other links in a transaction’s lifecycle. The Logos stack is engineered specifically to secure it all.</p><p>Rounding the event off on Saturday evening was the Logos LAN Party with local dev collective Bordel.
