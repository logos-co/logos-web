f:T5f82,<h2 id="testnet-v0-2-now-live">Testnet v0.2 now live</h2><p><a href="/media/article/testnet-v02-live">Logos Testnet v0.2 launched on 30 June</a> and, after several weeks of testing and hardening, we began its wider promotion last month.</p><p>The release strengthens the core Logos Messaging, Storage, and Blockchain modules while bringing foundational privacy features online. Key updates include the Blend Network for&nbsp;<a href="/media/article/why-proposer-anonymity">block proposer anonymity</a>, token bridging between sovereign Zones, decentralised sequencing, improved message delivery, and NAT traversal.</p><p>All core modules are now available through Logos Basecamp, making it easier to run a node, join the testnet, and start building privacy-preserving applications.</p><p><a href="/media/article/testnet-v02-architectural-readiness">Learn more</a> and run&nbsp;<a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://logos.co/get-started">Logos Basecamp</a>.</p><h2 id="bota-jardemalie-on-the-need-for-private-infrastructure">Bota Jardemalie&nbsp;on the need for private infrastructure</h2><p><img alt="bota-interview.png" src="/media-images/bota_interview_6ec8c9b853-b1f74807-750.webp" srcset="/media-images/bota_interview_6ec8c9b853-b1f74807-750.webp 750w, /media-images/bota_interview_6ec8c9b853-b1f74807-1400.webp 1400w" sizes="(max-width: 767px) calc(100vw - 32px), 700px" width="1440" height="800" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></p><p>In 2013, Kazakh lawyer Bota Jardemalie took a Skype call from a client whose testimony exposed how vulnerable communication channels can be. Soon after, she says she was publicly branded a criminal, placed on an Interpol red notice, targeted by surveillance and kidnapping attempts, and pressured through a years-long legal campaign and the detention of her brother.</p><p>Logos Broadcast Network spoke to Bota, who shared her story and reiterated the importance of secure communication channels:&nbsp;</p><blockquote><p>“If you don’t control your channel of communication, you become extremely vulnerable.”&nbsp;</p></blockquote><p>She also outlined the kind of tools and infrastructure our freedoms hinge on: private communication, censorship-resistant records, and payments that cannot be frozen or traced. We’re building Logos to protect people like Bota, as well as anyone else who might need private-by-default infrastructure.</p><div class="media-detail-video-frame"><div class="media-detail-video-aspect"><iframe title="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AtaMFMvpI1g" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/AtaMFMvpI1g" class="media-detail-video" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture" allowfullscreen></iframe></div></div><figure class="media"></figure><h2 id="medusa-wallet-live-on-logos">Medusa wallet live on Logos</h2><p>Medusa is a private, self-custodial wallet built for Logos Blockchain by developer ParadoxComputer. Available as a Logos Basecamp module, it supports multiple identities within one wallet and is designed to hold and move value across Logos zones.</p><p>ParadoxComputer describes it as a “MetaMask for Logos.”</p><p>In their own words:</p><blockquote><p>“One wallet, many identities. Hold and move value across every Logos zone with shielded, self-custodial privacy, and let dapps connect only with your say-so.”</p></blockquote><p>Medusa supports features including shielded transactions, private addresses and multiple-address management. It is part of ParadoxComputer’s wider development work on Logos, which also includes&nbsp;<a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://zonescan.paradox.computer/">LEZ ZoneScan</a>, an explorer for the Logos Execution Zone.</p><p><a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://github.com/paradoxcomputer/medusa">See the code</a> or&nbsp;<a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://medusa.paradox.computer/">check out Medusa on Logos now</a>.</p><p><i>Medusa wallet was developed independently using Logos tech. Do your own due diligence before installing.</i></p><h2 id="jarrad-hope-on-unchained">Jarrad Hope on Unchained</h2><p>Laura Shin’s&nbsp;<i>Unchained</i> podcast brought together Jarrad Hope of Logos, Joe Andrews of Aztec Labs, and Mert Mumtaz of Helius to discuss why privacy is becoming one of crypto’s most important frontiers. The conversation covered the challenges of building privacy technology, including Zcash’s long-undetected counterfeit vulnerability, the quantum threat to crypto, regulatory pressure, and the difference between cryptographic privacy and systems that still rely on trusted intermediaries.</p><p>Jarrad focused on privacy as a requirement for the entire transaction lifecycle. He argued that communication, network access, and other layers can expose users and institutions even when the blockchain itself offers strong settlement guarantees. He also introduced the audience to the Logos tech stack as a solution to leaks at links in the transaction supply chain beyond settlement.</p><div class="media-detail-video-frame"><div class="media-detail-video-aspect"><iframe title="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KSQt1PHwqiI" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/KSQt1PHwqiI" class="media-detail-video" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture" allowfullscreen></iframe></div></div><figure class="media"></figure><h2 id="irl-upcoming">IRL / Upcoming</h2><h3 id="logos-lan-and-circles-zanzalu">Logos LAN and Circles / Zanzalu</h3><p>Logos returned to Zanzalu in Zanzibar from 25 July to 14 August, building on the&nbsp;<a href="/media/article/logos-circles-winnable-issues">Circle formed there last year</a>. At the Logos booth, attendees can explore Basecamp, receive support with running a node, and learn how to participate in Logos Circle / Benin’s ongoing actions.
