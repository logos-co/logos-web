State of the Logos Network: October 2026

Your roundup of recent developments from the Logos social movement and tech stack

MovementCirclesLogos

Field Station builds the parallel in Rajasthan, Nedu’s story tackles corruption, Nick Merrill shares his plight, the Museum of Civil Liberties opens to community curation, and more. Logos Blog recalls the biggest movement news from October 2026.

Field Station / Rajasthan: A living experiment in parallel society building

In October, Logos and Zu-Grama brought 30 builders, researchers, activists, and community organisers together for Field Station / Rajasthan, an eight-day experiment in parallel organising at Dhun, a regenerative farm in Jaipur district. Through living, working, and building together, residents explored what a parallel society could look like in practice: communities with the tools and shared responsibility to organise, cooperate, and solve problems for themselves.

The residency connected that mission to local challenges, producing 15 projects, using Logos Basecamp, four winning prototypes and eight submissions for the Zu-Grama d/acc track. From the winners, Offline Zcash explores private payments without an internet connection; Bosphorus enables private lending inside Logos Basecamp; Pukaar lets residents report issues and verify completed repairs; and Dhun Commons provides a shared treasury and coordination tool for farmers and land stewards.

Each winning team will receive milestone-based grant funding and mentoring from Logos and Zu-Grama to further develop its prototype. Connecting the projects with Logos Circles will give local communities opportunities to help shape and use the tools, extending the experiment beyond the residency. The wider cohort will continue working together through reviews, monthly meet-ups, and shared follow-up builds.

You can learn more about Field Station / Rajasthan and the projects that emerged from it in upcoming articles and case studies on Logos Blog.

Nick Merrill: From Fighting Surveillance to Building Alternatives

Last month, we spoke with Nick Merrill, the internet service provider who challenged an FBI demand for customer records, resulting in a gag order that prevented him from discussing it. In the interview, Nick recounts his decade-long legal battle and how it shaped his work building tools to protect people’s privacy.

Surveillance agencies will keep seeking access to our data. Nick’s experience shows why privacy must be built into the infrastructure we use, limiting what can be collected, exposed, or handed over in the first place.

Read more about Nick’s story.

Experience corruption through Nedu’s choices  

“Life’s Choices” continues with Nedu, a young non-profit worker determined to improve his community in Lagos, Nigeria. You make his major decisions as a local clean up grows into an ambition to make changes at the national level, navigating demands for bribes, funding with strings attached, and pressure to stay quiet when money goes missing. His story explores how corruption constrains even our best intentions, and why we need systems that make abuse harder to conceal.

Nedu completes the Present chapter of the “Past. Present. Future.” initiative. Next, we look ahead to the Future, exploring the value of our money in the years to come. Look out for more information. 

For now, play Nedu’s story and see where your decisions take him.

Tech highlights

In October, the technical teams focused on strengthening and measuring what shipped with Testnet v0.3. After brief disruption to the testnet, decentralised sequencing was hardened to function better under restarts.

Benchmarks confirmed service discovery reliability, with services offered by only one node in 200 consistently discovered in under a second and a half. Separately, the price feed stayed within 0.5% of the market in 99.75% of cases across 20 days of data.

The Nimbos client also began sharing blocks and transactions across the network, a step towards having more than one independent implementation of the software running.

Contributors opened a discussion on how prepared Logos would be in the event that today’s cryptography is broken by quantum computing sooner than expected, responding to a call from the wider industry to take the risk seriously.

Stay tuned for the October Logos developer update, which will go into more detail and be live very soon.

Logos Circle / Kaduna welcomes students back to school

In October, Logos Circle / Kaduna completed its monthly winnable issue with a back-to-school outreach at Gadol-Chen Academy Nursery and Secondary Education. The Circle engaged more than 200 students and distributed over 200 exercise books, 50 maths sets, over 80 pairs of socks, 400 pens, and four whiteboards to support learning.

Alongside the materials, the Circle introduced students to Logos and discussed education, teamwork, and how people can work together to address community challenges. Coordinated and supported by DJFL Foundation, with additional writing materials from Pristine High School, the outreach brought together local partners to meet practical school needs and encourage students to take an active role in their communities.

More information.

Logos Circle / City Keffi expands access to menstrual health education

On 31 October, Logos Circle / Keffi held its Period with Dignity initiative in the Highcourt community in Keffi, Nasarawa State. Three Circle volunteers delivered sessions in English and Hausa for 35 teenage girls, covering menstrual health, hygiene, and common misconceptions. The outreach also included practical demonstrations of sanitary pad use and provided menstrual hygiene kits to every participant.

Open discussions gave participants space to raise personal concerns and ask things they had previously felt unable to discuss. Questions about affordable alternatives to disposable pads highlighted the financial barriers some girls face, reinforcing the need to combine education with practical support. In response to participants’ requests, the Circle plans to organise a follow-up session and seek partnerships with schools and healthcare professionals to sustain the initiative.
