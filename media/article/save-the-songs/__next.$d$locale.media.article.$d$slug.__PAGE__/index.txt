1b:T16a3,<h2 id="first-they-came-for-the-78s-and-i-did-not-speak-out">First they came for the 78s, and I did not speak out…</h2><p>In 2023, major record labels&nbsp;<a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2023/08/record-labels-sue-internet-archive-for-digitizing-obsolete-vintage-records/">sued</a> the not-for-profit Internet Archive for almost $400 million over its “<a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://great78.archive.org/donate-78s/">Great 78 Project</a>”. Since then, the labels have attempted to add 493 more recordings to the ongoing complaint, bringing the total in claimed damages to an absurd $621 million. If successful, the legal action threatens not only these rare historical sound recordings but the very existence of a piece of critical Internet infrastructure.</p><p>Internet Archive is a digital library that provides access to millions of cultural artefacts, including literature, audio, and video. The resource supports the research of authors and academics around the world. In the words of&nbsp;<a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://blog.archive.org/2023/03/03/author-the-internet-archive-is-the-most-useful-library/">author</a> Larry Dieterich:</p><p><i>“The Internet Archive is the most useful library. I honestly don’t think that the research I am doing would succeed without the resources provided by the Internet Archive.”</i></p><p>The Great 78 Project seeks to digitise some of the earliest recorded music, preserving it for generations to come. The initiative appealed for collectors to donate historic 78 rpm recordings for digital archiving. These artefacts allow researchers to cast an ear into the past. However, the increasingly rare shellac discs – precursors to the more durable vinyl used for later recordings – are extremely vulnerable to damage.</p><p>While the disappearance of these recordings would be a tragic loss for our cultural heritage, the collateral damage of the lawsuit would have much graver implications. Calling the action an “existential threat to the Internet Archive and everything we preserve”, Chris Freeland, the archive’s Director of Library Services,&nbsp;<a target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" href="https://blog.archive.org/2025/04/17/take-action-defend-the-internet-archive/">wrote</a> in an appeal to defend the project:&nbsp;</p><p><i>“At a time when digital information is disappearing, being rewritten, or erased entirely, the tools to preserve history must be defended – not dismantled.”</i></p><p>We call upon the Logos community to support the Internet Archive in its time of need. Because they are under centralised control, they are also vulnerable to the corruption and ineptitude of record keepers.”</i></p><p>Logos wholeheartedly supports the Internet Archive and similar efforts to prevent the eradication and distortion of our collective memory.
